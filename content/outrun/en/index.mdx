## Where your<br />miles go

Cheetahs don't die of slowness. They die of shrinking land, of farmers protecting their herds and of a pet trade that empties the Horn of Africa of cubs. Every pound you raise pays for the four things that turn that around.

<Fact icon="dog">
### Guardian dogs

An Anatolian shepherd raised with the herd cuts livestock losses by 80% or more. When the goats stop dying, the cheetahs stop being shot. One dog protects one farm for a decade.
</Fact>

<Fact icon="collar">
### GPS collars

A collar turns a rumour into a route. It shows which farms a cheetah crosses, where she raises her cubs and where the next corridor has to be, so land is protected where cheetahs actually go.
</Fact>

<Fact icon="scout">
### Community scouts

Local people, trained and paid, who count cheetahs, settle disputes over lost goats and raise the alarm when a cub goes missing. 77% of cheetah range lies outside any park. The scouts are the park.
</Fact>

<Fact icon="cub">
### The cub safe house

Cubs seized from traffickers arrive dehydrated and days from death. The safe house in Somaliland gives them vets, milk and a future, and has taken in more than a hundred since 2011.
</Fact>
</Raising>

## Know your<br />cheetah

<Feature image="features/cheetah/nought-to-sixty.webp" models="Solo, Coalition, Guardian" video="V8vejjVgIHg">
### Nought to sixty in three seconds

Quicker off the line than most cars, thanks to a spine that flexes like a bow, a tail that steers and claws that grip like running spikes.
</Feature>

<Feature image="features/cheetah/seven-metres.webp" models="Solo, Coalition, Guardian" video="uKH5qB_QR-E">
### Seven metres a stride

At full stretch a cheetah covers seven metres in one stride and is airborne for half of it. The sprint lasts about twenty seconds. Then it needs a sit-down.
</Feature>

<Feature image="features/cheetah/tear-marks.webp" models="Solo, Coalition, Guardian">
### The tear marks

The black lines from eye to mouth are thought to cut the glare of the midday sun, so the cheetah can hunt while the lions sleep.
</Feature>

<Feature image="features/cheetah/cant-roar.webp" models="Solo, Coalition, Guardian" video="uj_NyAGIg94">
### The cat that can't roar

Cheetahs chirp, purr and yelp. A mother calls her cubs with a chirp so bird-like that people walk straight past it.
</Feature>

<Feature image="features/cheetah/four-subspecies.webp" models="Solo, Coalition, Guardian">
### One species, four subspecies

Southern and eastern Africa hold nearly all of them. The Saharan cheetah is down to a few hundred. The Asiatic cheetah, fewer than fifty, all in Iran.
</Feature>

<Feature image="features/cheetah/india.webp" models="Solo, Coalition, Guardian" video="ZCrShncMeYE">
### India got its cheetahs back

India's last cheetahs were shot in 1947. Since 2022 it has released Namibian and South African cheetahs into Kuno National Park. Some died. Cubs were born. Recovery is possible, just slower than a cheetah.
</Feature>
</Features>
